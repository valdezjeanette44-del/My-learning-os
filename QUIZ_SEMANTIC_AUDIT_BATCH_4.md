- The map-app and paper-map questions correctly treat technology as tools and knowledge used to solve problems, not only electronic devices.
- Browser/server items correctly describe requests and responses over networks, without suggesting that a phone physically travels to a server or downloads the entire internet.
- Recipe and navigation examples correctly illustrate ordered algorithmic steps and how inputs or step order can affect results.
- AI questions appropriately warn that confident or fluent output can be wrong and should be checked against reliable sources or the original document.
- Distractors represent common misconceptions about technology, networks, algorithms, and AI reliability. No confirmed factual or answer-key error found.

## Philosophy
- Fairness questions encourage defining concepts, surfacing assumptions, and evaluating reasons rather than treating confidence or majority vote as proof.
- The lucky-guess question correctly distinguishes a true belief from a justified belief. The service-notice example appropriately considers evidence reliability and currency.
- The policy question correctly raises distribution of benefits and burdens, rights, and fairness.
- The one-town and one-online-course examples correctly identify the limits of generalizing from a single example and recommend broader evidence.
- Distractors reflect common reasoning errors: appeals to confidence or popularity, ignoring evidence quality, and overgeneralizing. No confirmed factual or answer-key error found.

## Disposition
No question wording or answer-key edits are recommended for Technology or Philosophy based on this pass. Preserve the prompts to verify AI output, assess evidence quality, and avoid overgeneralizing from isolated examples. This content review does not replace automated generation and live-app regression tests.
